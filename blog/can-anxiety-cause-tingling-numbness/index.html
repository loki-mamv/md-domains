Can Anxiety Cause Tingling or Numbness in Your Hands and Face?

September 28, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Yes. Anxiety, and panic attacks in particular, can cause real tingling or numbness in the hands, fingertips, and around the mouth. The main driver is fast, deep breathing, which lowers carbon dioxide in the blood and makes nerves more excitable. The feeling usually fades as breathing settles. Sudden numbness on one side of the face or body, with weakness, is a stroke warning sign and needs 911.

Why does a panic attack make your hands and face tingle?

The link is breathing. During a surge of fear, many people breathe faster and deeper than the body needs. MedlinePlus calls this hyperventilation, or overbreathing. It lists anxiety, nervousness, panic attacks, and stress as common triggers.

Overbreathing blows off carbon dioxide faster than the body makes it. With less carbon dioxide, the blood becomes more alkaline. Doctors call this respiratory alkalosis.

The shift in pH has a knock-on effect on calcium. A StatPearls review of alkalosis explains that a higher pH makes more calcium bind to albumin, a blood protein. That leaves less free (ionized) calcium in circulation. Low ionized calcium makes nerves fire too easily, which shows up as tingling (paresthesias) and cramping of the hands and feet (carpopedal spasm).

Your total calcium does not drop. The calcium is still there, just temporarily bound. That is why the tingling goes away once breathing slows and carbon dioxide returns to normal.

Where does anxiety tingling usually show up?

The classic pattern is tingling in the fingertips and around the lips. MedlinePlus lists "numbness and tingling in the arms or around the mouth" and muscle spasms in the hands and feet among hyperventilation symptoms. Other symptoms often come with it:

  • Lightheadedness or a feeling of being about to faint
  • Chest tightness or chest pain
  • A feeling of not getting enough air, even while breathing hard
  • Dry mouth
  • Hands that stiffen or curl in a cramp

Most people feel it on both sides. But not always. In a Norwegian study of 25 patients with hyperventilation syndrome referred to neurologists, 8 described numbness or tingling that was worse on one side. The authors noted that sensory symptoms "may be asymmetric and mimic focal cerebral disease." So one-sided tingling does not prove anxiety is not the cause, and it does not prove it is. It needs a medical check.

Tingling is only one of many body signals anxiety can produce. Our overview of physical symptoms of anxiety covers the wider list.

Is numbness an official panic attack symptom?

Yes. The DSM-5 defines a panic attack as an abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort that peaks within minutes. According to the StatPearls chapter on panic disorder, four or more of a set list of symptoms must be present. That list includes "paresthesias (numbness or tingling sensations)," along with a pounding heart, sweating, shaking, shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, and fear of dying.

The National Institute of Mental Health also lists "tingly or numb hands" as a panic attack symptom. It notes that attacks can last from a few minutes to an hour or sometimes longer, and can even happen during sleep.

This matters because the tingling often feeds the fear. A person feels numb lips, thinks "stroke," breathes even faster, and the tingling gets worse. If you are unsure whether what you had was a panic attack, see panic attack vs anxiety attack.

How can you tell anxiety tingling from stroke, MS, or nerve problems?

Many conditions cause numbness and tingling. MedlinePlus lists diabetes, carpal tunnel syndrome, multiple sclerosis, stroke, low B vitamins (B1, B6, B12, or folic acid), and abnormal calcium, potassium, or sodium levels, among others. Timing and pattern give the best clues, but they do not replace an exam.

  • Stroke: The CDC lists "sudden numbness or weakness in the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side." Look for a drooping face, an arm that drifts down, slurred speech, vision loss, or loss of balance. These do not ease with slow breathing.
  • Carpal tunnel syndrome: MedlinePlus describes numbness or tingling in the thumb and the next two or three fingers. It tends to follow the median nerve, not the whole hand, and it does not affect the lips.
  • Diabetes and B12 deficiency: Nerve damage from these tends to build slowly. It is often present most days, not only during a scare.
  • Multiple sclerosis: Numbness from MS can last days or weeks. It is not tied to a burst of fear and fast breathing.

Anxiety tingling usually has a clear shape. It starts during or right after a surge of fear, comes with fast breathing, affects the lips and fingertips, and fades within minutes to an hour as you calm down.

What should you do when the tingling starts?

If you have any stroke sign, call 911 first. Do not wait to see if it passes. If you have had this pattern before and a doctor has linked it to panic, these steps can help:

  1. Slow the out-breath. MedlinePlus recommends pursed-lip breathing: breathe in through the nose, then out slowly through lips shaped as if to whistle.
  2. Breathe from the belly. Diaphragm breathing moves less air per minute than fast chest breathing. That lets carbon dioxide build back up.
  3. Name what is happening. Tell yourself the tingling comes from breathing, not nerve damage. Reassurance from someone calm also helps, per MedlinePlus.
  4. Wait it out. The tingling usually fades as breathing slows. Checking your pulse or testing your grip over and over tends to keep the fear going.

When should you see a doctor about tingling?

MedlinePlus advises seeking care if you have rapid breathing for the first time, since it may be an emergency. Call 911 if numbness comes with weakness, confusion, slurred speech, vision change, trouble walking, loss of bladder or bowel control, or a recent head, neck, or back injury.

Book a routine visit if tingling happens without a clear trigger, lasts between attacks, stays in one hand or foot, or gets worse over time. Simple blood tests can check B12, blood sugar, and calcium.

If attacks keep coming back, treatment works. The NIMH calls cognitive behavioral therapy the "gold standard" for panic disorder. It teaches people to react differently to the body sensations of fear, including tingling. Medicines such as SSRIs and SNRIs are also used. The bottom line

Anxiety can cause real tingling and numbness, most often in the fingertips and around the mouth. Fast breathing lowers carbon dioxide, the blood turns more alkaline, and less free calcium reaches the nerves. It is on the official panic attack symptom list and it fades as breathing slows. Sudden one-sided numbness, weakness, face droop, or slurred speech is different: call 911. Tingling that lingers between attacks deserves a doctor visit to rule out B12 deficiency, diabetes, and nerve compression.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does tingling from a panic attack last?

It usually lasts as long as the fast breathing does. The NIMH says panic attacks last from a few minutes to an hour or sometimes longer. Once breathing slows and carbon dioxide recovers, the tingling tends to fade.

Can anxiety cause numbness on only one side of the body?

It can. In one neurology study, 8 of 25 people with hyperventilation syndrome had numbness worse on one side. Because one-sided numbness is also a stroke sign, it should always be checked by a doctor, and sudden cases need 911.

Why do my hands cramp or curl during a panic attack?

Overbreathing makes the blood more alkaline, which lowers free calcium. Low free calcium makes nerves and muscles overexcitable, causing hand and foot spasms. Slowing your breathing usually lets the cramping ease.

Should I breathe into a paper bag?

MedlinePlus advises pursed-lip breathing and belly breathing for hyperventilation, not bag breathing. Slow, controlled breaths help carbon dioxide return to normal. If this is your first episode of rapid breathing, seek medical care.

Can low vitamin B12 feel like anxiety tingling?

Low B12 can cause numbness and tingling, often in the hands and feet. It tends to build slowly and last most days rather than come and go with fear. A simple blood test can check your level.
