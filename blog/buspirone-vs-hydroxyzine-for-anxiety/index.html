Buspirone vs Hydroxyzine for Anxiety: Which Works Faster and Which Is for Daily Use?

September 15, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Hydroxyzine works faster. Its FDA label says effects usually show within 15 to 30 minutes, so it suits short-term, as-needed relief. Buspirone takes about 2 to 4 weeks to help and must be taken every day, so it fits ongoing generalized anxiety. Hydroxyzine brings more drowsiness and heart-rhythm cautions, and older adults are usually told to avoid it.

How do buspirone and hydroxyzine work differently?

These two drugs share almost nothing except the goal of easing anxiety. They act on different brain systems, and that explains most of the gap in speed and side effects.

Hydroxyzine is a first-generation antihistamine. MedlinePlus says it blocks histamine and decreases activity in the brain. The FDA-approved label describes it as unrelated to benzodiazepines. It may act by quieting certain areas below the brain's cortex. It also has antihistamine, anti-nausea, and mild muscle-relaxing effects.

Buspirone is an anxiolytic in the azapirone class. Its label says the exact mechanism is unknown. It binds strongly to serotonin 5-HT1A receptors and moderately to dopamine D2 receptors. It has no meaningful effect on benzodiazepine or GABA receptors. The label adds that it lacks the strong sedation of typical anxiety drugs.

What is each one FDA-approved for?

The labels are worded differently, and the wording matters.

  • Hydroxyzine: "symptomatic relief of anxiety and tension associated with psychoneurosis and as an adjunct in organic disease states in which anxiety is manifested." It is also approved for itching from allergic skin conditions and as a sedative around surgery.
  • Buspirone: "the management of anxiety disorders or the short-term relief of the symptoms of anxiety." Its efficacy trials enrolled outpatients whose diagnosis roughly matched generalized anxiety disorder (GAD).

Both labels hedge on long-term use. Hydroxyzine's label says its effectiveness for more than 4 months "has not been assessed by systematic clinical studies." Buspirone's label says controlled trials have not shown effectiveness beyond 3 to 4 weeks. It notes, however, that 264 patients took it for 1 year without ill effect. Both labels tell doctors to reassess usefulness periodically.

If you are not sure whether your worry fits GAD, our guide to generalized anxiety disorder symptoms walks through the criteria.

Which one works faster?

Hydroxyzine, by a wide margin. The label states it is rapidly absorbed, with clinical effects usually noted within 15 to 30 minutes of an oral dose. That is why it is often prescribed for a tense afternoon or a hard evening.

Buspirone is absorbed quickly too, with peak blood levels 40 to 90 minutes after a dose. The calming effect lags far behind. A StatPearls review on NCBI Bookshelf says it "has little efficacy as an acute anxiolytic" because the clinical effect typically takes 2 to 4 weeks. Taking an extra tablet during a panic spike will not help. For how this compares with SSRIs and other classes, see how long anxiety medication takes to work.

Which one is meant for daily use?

Buspirone is built for a daily schedule. MedlinePlus describes twice-daily dosing, taken consistently either always with food or always without. The label explains why: food raised peak levels of unchanged buspirone by 116% in a small study. Doses are raised gradually, no more often than every 2 to 3 days.

Hydroxyzine's label lists dosing up to four times a day, and its fast onset also makes as-needed use possible. The evidence for steady long-term use is thin. A Cochrane review of hydroxyzine for GAD found only 5 usable trials with 884 participants. It rated the risk of bias as high. The authors concluded hydroxyzine cannot be recommended as a reliable first-line GAD treatment.

Buspirone has a larger evidence base. A Cochrane review of azapirones pooled 36 trials with 5,908 participants. Azapirones beat placebo, with a number needed to treat of 4.4. Most trials lasted only 4 to 9 weeks, and the authors called for longer studies. In the small head-to-head data, hydroxyzine and buspirone showed no clear efficacy difference (odds ratio 0.76, 95% CI 0.40 to 1.42).

How do the side effects and safety warnings compare?

Hydroxyzine carries the heavier warning list:

  • Drowsiness. The label warns against driving until you know your response. The Cochrane review found more sleepiness than with comparison drugs (odds ratio 1.74).
  • QT prolongation. The label reports post-marketing cases of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes. It is contraindicated with a known prolonged QT interval. Caution applies with heart disease, electrolyte problems, and QT-prolonging drugs such as citalopram, azithromycin, methadone, and ondansetron.
  • Anticholinergic effects. Dry mouth is the listed effect. Alcohol and other sedatives add to its impact.
  • Pregnancy. The label lists early pregnancy as a contraindication. It also says not to give it to nursing mothers.
  • Rare skin reaction. Acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) can occur. Stop the drug at the first sign of a rash.

Buspirone is less sedating, according to its label. It carries different warnings. It must not be combined with MAO inhibitors, including linezolid and IV methylene blue, because of serotonin syndrome and blood pressure risk. Restlessness shortly after starting has been reported in a small fraction of patients. Grapefruit juice in large amounts should be avoided.

Who does each one suit, and what about dependence?

Neither drug is a benzodiazepine. Buspirone's label states it is not a controlled substance. In human and animal studies it showed no abuse potential, tolerance, or physical dependence. One practical catch: it does not prevent benzodiazepine withdrawal, so those drugs need a gradual taper first. Hydroxyzine's label has no drug abuse and dependence section, but it does warn that it adds to the effects of alcohol and other sedatives.

In broad terms, the evidence points this way:

  • Buspirone fits people with ongoing, day-to-day GAD who can wait a few weeks. It also fits people who want to avoid sedation or dependence risk.
  • Hydroxyzine fits younger, healthy adults who need occasional fast relief. It also suits people whose anxiety comes with itching or poor sleep, when drowsiness is acceptable.

Age changes the picture. The 2023 American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria list hydroxyzine among first-generation antihistamines to avoid in adults 65 and older (strong recommendation). The panel cites confusion, constipation, and higher risk of falls, delirium, and dementia from anticholinergic drugs. MedlinePlus adds that older adults usually should not take hydroxyzine.

If anxiety is mainly keeping you awake, insomnia.md covers sleep-focused options. A sedating antihistamine is rarely the best long-term sleep plan.

The bottom line

Hydroxyzine calms within 15 to 30 minutes but brings drowsiness, QT warnings, and a Beers Criteria "avoid" rating for older adults. Buspirone needs 2 to 4 weeks of daily use but causes little sedation and no known dependence. Neither label has strong evidence for years of use, so plan regular check-ins with your prescriber. For other long-term options, ask about therapy and antidepressants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take buspirone only when I feel anxious?

Buspirone is not designed for as-needed use. Its calming effect builds over about 2 to 4 weeks of steady daily dosing, so a single dose during a spike of anxiety does little. Ask your prescriber about a different plan if you need fast relief.

Is hydroxyzine addictive like Xanax?

Hydroxyzine is an antihistamine, not a benzodiazepine, and its FDA label carries no dependence warning. It still causes drowsiness and adds to the effects of alcohol and other sedatives. Use it only as prescribed.

Can buspirone and hydroxyzine be taken together?

Neither label lists the other drug as a contraindication. Hydroxyzine's label does advise caution with other drugs that act on the brain. Only combine them if your prescriber sets it up and checks your heart history and other medicines.

Does hydroxyzine affect the heart?

Its label reports post-marketing cases of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm. It is contraindicated in people with a known prolonged QT interval. Tell your doctor about heart disease, fainting, or other QT-prolonging drugs before starting it.

Why do older adults get told to avoid hydroxyzine?

The 2023 AGS Beers Criteria rate hydroxyzine as a drug to avoid in adults 65 and older. It is strongly anticholinergic and clears more slowly with age. That raises the risk of confusion, falls, and delirium.
